## Lesson Arc

- Negative exponents become positive when the factor moves across the fraction bar.
- `x^-3` becomes `1 / x^3`; `x^-2` in the denominator becomes `x^2` in the numerator.
- For this course, answers should not be left with negative exponents.
- The move works for multiplied factors, not for terms joined by addition/subtraction.
- Prior exponent rules still apply: add exponents when multiplying like bases, subtract when dividing, multiply powers when raising a power to a power.
- After simplifying with exponent rules, move any remaining negative-exponent factors to the other side of the fraction.

## Teacher Language Worth Preserving

- "If it's on the bottom, it goes to the top and it becomes positive."
- "If it's on the top, it goes to the bottom and it becomes positive."
- "Plus or minus has ruined the party."
- "We can't have a negative exponent in the answer."
- "All the rules from before still go into play."
- "PEMDAS: parentheses comes first."

## Misconceptions to Build Tools Around

- Students may try to distribute the negative-exponent move across addition/subtraction.
- Students may think the negative sign makes the value negative, instead of meaning reciprocal.
- Students may move an entire sum/difference instead of only a multiplied factor.
- Students may simplify exponent rules correctly but forget the final "no negative exponent" cleanup.
- Students may confuse subtraction of exponents with subtracting a negative exponent.

## Tool Improvements for Negative Exponent Flipper

- Add a "factor vs term" mode:
  - Valid: `x^-2 y^3 -> y^3 / x^2`
  - Invalid: `x^-2 + y^3` cannot be flipped as one fraction because addition/subtraction separates terms.
- Add a "cleanup after rules" mini-step:
  - Example: `x^-11 * x^4 -> x^-7 -> 1/x^7`
  - Example: `x^-4 / x^-13 -> x^9`
- Add a "top or bottom?" drag interaction where students move only the negative-exponent factor across the fraction bar.
- Add a warning callout when a plus/minus appears between terms: "plus/minus ruins the party."

## Tool Prompt Ideas

- Move `x^-3` to make the exponent positive. What changed: the sign, the location, or both?
- Why is `x^-2 + y^3` not the same kind of move as `x^-2 y^3`?
- Simplify `x^-11 * x^4`. What happens before the factor moves?
- Simplify `x^-4 / x^-13`. Why does the answer stay on top?
- Build an expression that ends with `1/x^18`.

## Related Site Updates

- The existing Negative Exponent Flipper should become more than a one-step animation; it should show the sequence:
  1. Identify factors.
  2. Apply exponent rules if like bases are multiplied/divided.
  3. Move negative-exponent factors across the fraction bar.
  4. Confirm all exponents are positive.
