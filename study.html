UNIT 1
Systems & Quadratics
Sept 2 – Sept 26  ·  15 tools
KEY IDEAS
  • ①A system's solution is the point where both graphs cross — it satisfies both equations simultaneously.
  • ②Substitution: isolate one variable, plug into the other equation, solve, then back-substitute for the second variable.
  • ③Elimination: multiply rows to align a coefficient, add/subtract to cancel a variable, solve, then back-substitute.
  • ④The discriminant b²−4ac tells you the number of solutions before you solve: >0 → two real; =0 → one real; <0 → two imaginary.
  • ⑤For absolute value: |expr| = k splits into two equations: expr = k AND expr = −k.
FORMULA SHEET
QUADRATIC FORMULA
x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / (2a)
Works for any ax²+bx+c = 0. The ± gives both roots.
VERTEX COORDINATES
h = −b/(2a)
k = f(h)
Plug h back into the original equation to get k.
VERTEX FORM
y = a(x−h)² + k
Vertex is at (h, k). Opens up if a > 0, down if a < 0.
DISCRIMINANT
Δ = b² − 4ac
Δ > 0: two real roots  ·  Δ = 0: one root  ·  Δ < 0: imaginary
ABSOLUTE VALUE EQUATION
|expr| = k
→ expr = k or expr = −k
⚠ If k < 0, no solution — absolute value can't be negative.
INEQUALITY — FLIP RULE
Multiply or divide by a
negative → flip the sign
−2x > 6 → x < −3. Sign flips when dividing by −2.

UNIT 2
Graphs & Exponents
Sept 29 – Oct 17  ·  7 tools
KEY IDEAS
  • ①Every transformation follows y = a·f(b(x−h)) + k. |a| stretches/reflects vertically, b compresses horizontally, (h,k) shifts.
  • ②Exponent rules only apply when the base is the same — you can't combine x³ · y² with a product rule.
  • ③Negative exponent = move the base to the other side of the fraction bar. x⁻³ = 1/x³. The sign does NOT stay negative.
  • ④Difference of squares: a²−b² = (a+b)(a−b). Look for perfect square terms with a minus between them.
FORMULA SHEET
PRODUCT RULE
xᵃ · xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ
Same base → ADD exponents
QUOTIENT RULE
xᵃ / xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ
Same base → SUBTRACT (top minus bottom)
POWER RULE
(xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃᵇ
Power of a power → MULTIPLY exponents
ZERO EXPONENT
x⁰ = 1
Any nonzero base to the 0 power equals 1.
NEGATIVE EXPONENT
x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ
⚠ Result is a fraction, NOT a negative number.
TRANSFORMATION TEMPLATE
y = a·f(b(x−h)) + k
|a|: stretch; b: horiz. compress; h: right; k: up
DIFFERENCE OF SQUARES
a² − b² = (a+b)(a−b)
Must be subtraction — sum of squares doesn't factor.

UNIT 3
Rationals & Complex
Oct 20 – Nov 7  ·  9 tools
KEY IDEAS
  • ①i = √(−1) and i² = −1. Powers of i cycle: i¹=i, i²=−1, i³=−i, i⁴=1, then repeat.
  • ②To complete the square: take half the x-coefficient, square it, add and subtract that value to force a perfect square trinomial.
  • ③A rational expression's domain excludes every value that makes the denominator equal 0 — find them before you simplify.
  • ④Conjugate pairs (a+bi)(a−bi) = a²+b² — always a real number. Used when dividing complex numbers.
FORMULA SHEET
POWERS OF i
i¹=i   i²=−1   i³=−i   i⁴=1
Cycle repeats every 4. Divide the exponent by 4 — the remainder tells you which power.
COMPLEX ADDITION
(a+bi)+(c+di) = (a+c)+(b+d)i
Group real parts together, imaginary parts together.
COMPLEX MULTIPLY (FOIL)
Real: ac − bd
Imaginary: ad + bc
FOIL then replace i² with −1. That last term flips sign.
COMPLETE THE SQUARE
x² + bx
→ (x + b/2)² − (b/2)²
Add (b/2)² to create the square, subtract it to keep balance.
VERTEX FROM STANDARD FORM
h = −b/(2a)
k = c − b²/(4a)
Or just plug h back into the equation to find k.
CONJUGATE PRODUCT
(a+bi)(a−bi) = a² + b²
Result is always real — i terms cancel. Key for dividing complex numbers.

UNIT 4
Rational Exponents
Nov 10 – Dec 5  ·  7 tools
KEY IDEAS
  • ①In x^(m/n), the denominator is the root and the numerator is the power: x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)ᵐ.
  • ②Composition: (f∘g)(x) = f(g(x)) — evaluate g first, feed the result into f. Order matters: f∘g ≠ g∘f usually.
  • ③To find an inverse function: swap x and y in the equation, then solve for the new y.
  • ④Radical equations: isolate the radical, raise both sides to the nth power, then always check for extraneous solutions.
FORMULA SHEET
RATIONAL EXPONENT — ROOT FORM
x^(1/n) = ⁿ√x
Denominator = the index of the radical.
RATIONAL EXPONENT — FULL FORM
x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)ᵐ = ⁿ√(xᵐ)
Root first (usually easier), then power. Or power first, then root — same answer.
COMPOSITION
(f∘g)(x) = f( g(x) )
Right function goes in first. (f∘g) ≠ (g∘f) in general.
INVERSE FUNCTION
1. Write y = f(x)
2. Swap x and y
3. Solve for y → f⁻¹(x)
The graph of f⁻¹ is f reflected over y = x.
RADICAL EQUATION STEPS
isolate → raise both sides to n → solve → CHECK
⚠ Extraneous solutions are common. Always sub back in.
DOMAIN OF √(expr)
expr ≥ 0
Set the radicand ≥ 0 and solve — that's the domain.

UNIT 5
Exponential & Log
January – February  ·  7 tools
KEY IDEAS
  • ①log_b(x) = y ↔ b^y = x: logs and exponentials are inverses. Switch between them to solve.
  • ②To solve exponential equations: make the bases the same (equate exponents), OR take log of both sides.
  • ③Growth: b > 1. Decay: 0 < b < 1. The initial value a = y when x = 0.
  • ④Log properties let you pull exponents out front — that's how you solve for a variable trapped in an exponent.
FORMULA SHEET
LOG DEFINITION
log_b(x) = y ↔ bʸ = x
Read: "b to the y equals x." These forms are identical.
EXPONENTIAL MODEL
y = a · bˣ
a = starting value (x=0), b = growth/decay multiplier per step
COMPOUND INTEREST
A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)
P = principal, r = annual rate, n = times/year, t = years
CONTINUOUS GROWTH
A = Pe^(rt)
e ≈ 2.718. Use when compounding is continuous.
LOG PRODUCT RULE
log(xy) = log x + log y
LOG QUOTIENT RULE
log(x/y) = log x − log y
LOG POWER RULE
log(xⁿ) = n · log x
This is how you get the variable out of an exponent.
CHANGE OF BASE
log_b(x) = ln(x) / ln(b)
Use when your calculator doesn't have log_b directly.

UNIT 6
Rationals & Polynomials
February – March  ·  8 tools
KEY IDEAS
  • ①To simplify a rational: factor numerator AND denominator, cancel common factors, state excluded values for every canceled factor.
  • ②Vertical asymptotes come from denominator factors that didn't cancel. Holes come from factors that did cancel.
  • ③Horizontal asymptote: top degree < bottom → y=0; equal degrees → ratio of leading coefficients; top > bottom → no H.A. (slant asymptote).
  • ④To add/subtract rationals: factor denominators, build the LCD, rewrite each fraction, combine numerators, simplify.
FORMULA SHEET
SIMPLIFY RATIONAL
factor → cancel → state x ≠ excluded values
⚠ State the excluded values BEFORE canceling.
VERTICAL ASYMPTOTE
Set remaining denominator = 0
After full simplification — don't include factors that canceled (those are holes).
HOLE LOCATION
x-val: set canceled factor = 0
y-val: sub x into simplified form
HORIZONTAL ASYMPTOTE
deg(top) < deg(bot) → y = 0
deg equal → y = a/b (leading coeffs)
deg(top) > deg(bot) → none
ADD RATIONALS — LCD METHOD
1. Factor denoms
2. LCD = all unique factors
3. Rewrite fractions
4. Combine numerators
POLYNOMIAL LONG DIVISION
dividend = (divisor)(quotient) + remainder
Divide lead term → multiply → subtract → bring down → repeat

UNIT 7
Trigonometry
March – April  ·  7 tools
KEY IDEAS
  • ①On the unit circle, the coordinates at angle θ are (cos θ, sin θ). Know the 16 key angles.
  • ②Trig graph parameters follow the same a, b, h, k template: y = a·sin(b(x−h)) + k.
  • ③SOH CAH TOA only works with right triangles. Label opposite/adjacent relative to the reference angle.
  • ④Coterminal angles differ by 360° (or 2π). Negative angles go clockwise. Radians and degrees are related by π/180.
FORMULA SHEET
SOH CAH TOA
sin = opp/hyp
cos = adj/hyp
tan = opp/adj
UNIT CIRCLE
point at angle θ = (cos θ, sin θ)
x-coordinate is cosine, y-coordinate is sine. Radius = 1.
SINE / COSINE GRAPH
y = a·sin(b(x−h)) + k
AMPLITUDE
|a|
Distance from midline to max (or min). Always positive.
PERIOD
T = 2π / |b|
How long one full cycle takes. Larger b → shorter period.
PHASE SHIFT
h (to the right if h > 0)
⚠ In y=sin(bx−c), the shift is c/b not c.
MIDLINE
y = k
The horizontal center of the wave. Vertical shift amount.
PYTHAGOREAN IDENTITY
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
DEGREE ↔ RADIAN
π rad = 180°
multiply by π/180 to convert

UNIT 8
Statistics, Probability & Conics
April – May  ·  4 tools
KEY IDEAS
  • ①IQR = Q3 − Q1. Outlier fences: below Q1 − 1.5·IQR or above Q3 + 1.5·IQR.
  • ②P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). If mutually exclusive, P(A and B)=0 so you just add.
  • ③In conic equations, (h, k) is always the center — or vertex for parabolas. The signs in (x−h)² and (y−k)² are always opposite to h and k.
  • ④A circle has r² on the right. An ellipse has two different denominators and sums to 1. A hyperbola subtracts and sums to 1.
FORMULA SHEET
5-NUMBER SUMMARY
min · Q1 · median · Q3 · max
Put data in order first. Median splits; Q1 and Q3 split each half.
IQR & OUTLIER FENCES
IQR = Q3 − Q1
Lower: Q1 − 1.5·IQR
Upper: Q3 + 1.5·IQR
ADDITION RULE
P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B)
Subtract the overlap so you don't count it twice.
COMPLEMENT RULE
P(Aᶜ) = 1 − P(A)
P("not A") = 1 minus P(A). All probabilities sum to 1.
CIRCLE
(x−h)² + (y−k)² = r²
Center (h,k), radius r. Signs in equation are opposite to h and k.
PARABOLA
y = a(x−h)² + k
Vertex (h,k). Same as quadratic vertex form.
ELLIPSE
(x−h)²/a² + (y−k)²/b² = 1
Center (h,k). Longer axis = larger denominator.
HYPERBOLA
(x−h)²/a² − (y−k)²/b² = 1
Subtraction (not addition). Opens left/right if x is first.
